Why I made it

I wanted local dictation and call transcription on my Mac that is fast, simple and robust. Local transcription on Apple silicon is so good now that Mostly. Local models still slip when you switch languages mid-sentence. They also do worse in languages Whisper knows less well. A noisy room trips up every model, cloud ones included. process your data in the cloud. Handy and OpenSuperWhisper are my all-time favorites for local dictation, and I've learned a lot from them.

I spent a lot of time using those tools and ran into a few problems. Sometimes I'd talk and the mic was off, so Dictate shows a level meter and your words as you talk. Sometimes I'd hit Escape by accident and lose what I'd said, so only Dictate's own shortcut stops a recording.

The biggest thing, though, was calls. I wanted the same setup to work for my online calls, reliably. I used to record them on my phone, which meant keeping the call on speaker. So I built Blend Mic, a virtual mic that also hears your Mac's sound. That let me pass a call into tools that already transcribe well, like dembrane, Handy and OpenSuperWhisper.

People kept asking me about the setup, mostly people who didn't want to download speech models and set up a virtual mic themselves. So I made the whole thing easy to set up and put it out there. Dictate has Blend Mic's audio engine built in, and no bot joins the call. Your agents can read all of it.

Questions

Should I ask before I transcribe a call?

Yes. Ask before you press Record, and don't record if they say no. People do mind, and with no bot in the call they only know if you tell them. You could say: "Can I transcribe this for my notes? It stays on my Mac and the audio is deleted within a week." How to record calls with consent.

For me it's personal. I'm not a native English speaker, and recording lets me stay present in the call instead of scrambling for notes. I take my notes afterwards.

By default the audio is deleted after 7 days and the transcript after 30. In the Data retention settings you can delete the audio as soon as it's transcribed, or after 1 day.

Does anything leave my Mac?

Your audio and transcripts stay on your Mac in one folder. By default the audio is deleted after 7 days and the transcript after 30. You can delete the audio once it's transcribed or after 1 day, or keep it forever in developer mode. History can be kept for 7 days, 30 days or forever. Dictate goes online once for its speech models (1.6 GB) and later for updates. The update server counts checks by app, version and country. It never stores your IP address. If you connect an agent it can read your transcripts. Where it sends them depends on that agent. Where everything is kept

What will it cost?

Free forever for the first 50 early access users. The next 50 pay $25, then a lifetime license is $49.

How is it different from Handy and OpenSuperWhisper?

At the core they're close, and I learned a lot from both. All three run on your Mac. Handy also runs Parakeet and works on Windows and Linux. Dictate adds three things: retries on unsure words, online calls with both sides kept apart and a local MCP server for your agents.

Is it open source?

No. It is source available under FSL-1.1-MIT. You can read, change and run the code. You cannot offer it in a commercial product that competes with it. Each version becomes MIT two years after release, and versions up to 0.11.0 already are. The code and license are on GitHub.

Why do I need to activate it?

Dictate is free, and I want to know who uses it. Your email gets the code, and I might write to hear how you use Dictate and what's missing. Tell me to stop and I will. The app does not sign in and does not report what you do.

What does it ask permission for?

Microphone, to hear you. Accessibility, to paste into the app you are in. Without it your text waits on the clipboard. Call audio, to hear the other side of an online call. It records only the call app's sound. You can skip it and still dictate.

What can my agent see?

Your dictations and call transcripts, through the tools you leave on. The server listens only on your Mac and needs the token from the Agents settings. Tools that record or change anything start off. Your agent reads what the other side of a call says, and that could steer it.

I use OpenSuperWhisper, which also uses Option+`. Will they clash?

Yes. Change the shortcut in one of them. In Dictate it is in the Shortcuts settings. If OpenSuperWhisper already has the speech model, Dictate reuses it instead of downloading 1.6 GB again.

Which call apps work?

Zoom, Teams, Slack, Discord, FaceTime, WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, Webex, Skype and Element. Calls in a browser tab such as Meet work too. How to transcribe calls on a Mac.

Can I use it on my phone?

Not yet. I record the call as a voice memo on my Apple Watch. Then I import the file into Dictate on my Mac.

Which languages are supported?

English works best. Whisper also does well in Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Japanese, Chinese and Korean. Other languages work too, with more mistakes. For those, pick Large v3 in the Model settings.

Why Whisper and not Parakeet?

Retries use the confidence Whisper gives each part it writes, and key terms go into Whisper's prompt. Parakeet is faster, and I have not measured it against this pipeline.
