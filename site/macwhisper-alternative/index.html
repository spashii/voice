A MacWhisper alternative for dictation and calls

MacWhisper is excellent. It runs on your Mac, transcribes almost any file, and since version 15.2 it shows a live transcript of your meetings.

I wanted fast, simple and robust dictation and transcription for calls that runs on my Mac without sending data to the cloud. That became Dictate. It does less than MacWhisper, on purpose: it's for what you say and the calls you're on, and your agent can use both.

What Dictate does differently

  • Your side of a call is always labeled Me. Dictate records your microphone and the call's audio separately, so it never has to guess which voice is yours.
  • Your agent can work with all of it. Claude Code or any MCP client can search your dictations and calls, and follow a call while it happens.
  • Unsure parts get a second try. Whisper scores how sure it is of each part, and Dictate decodes the unsure ones again and keeps the better version.
  • You can see it's working. A level meter shows the mic hears you, and your words appear as you talk. Only Dictate's own shortcut stops a recording, so a stray Escape never loses what you said.
  • Audio doesn't pile up. By default Dictate deletes audio after 7 days and transcripts after 30. You can have the audio deleted as soon as it's transcribed.

Where MacWhisper is the better pick

MacWhisper is the one for files: batch jobs, watched folders, YouTube links and exports such as SRT and DOCX. It tells up to eight speakers apart, offers more models and languages, writes summaries with the AI provider you choose, and runs on Intel Macs. If that's what you need, it's great at it.
